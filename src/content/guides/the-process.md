From here the process runs on statutory clocks. Miss one and the claim is usually lost not
argued about, lost and you have to start again. Diary every date the day the notice goes out.

## Notice of invitation to participate

Before the claim notice, the RTM company must give a notice of invitation to participate to
every qualifying tenant who is not already a member. The claim notice cannot be given until at
least 14 days after that.

The notice has prescribed contents. It has to state that the company intends to acquire the
right to manage, invite the recipient to become a member, and tell them how to find out more.
Companies that skip this step because "everyone already knows" hand the landlord a clean
technical defence.

Keep proof of service for each one: who it went to, at what address, on what date, by what
method.

## The claim notice

The claim notice is the formal claim. It goes to the landlord, to any party to the leases other
than the landlord and tenant (a management company named in the lease, typically), and to any
manager appointed by the tribunal. Every member of the RTM company must be given a copy.

It must specify the premises, state the grounds on which the building qualifies, list the
members and their flats, and set out two dates: the date by which a counter-notice may be given,
and the proposed acquisition date.

**Getting the addresses right matters.** Serve on the landlord's address as registered at HM
Land Registry, and check the title again shortly before service freeholds change hands, and
service on a former owner is not service.

## The landlord's counter-notice

The landlord has one month from the claim notice to give a counter-notice. There are two
possibilities.

If the counter-notice admits the claim, or if no counter-notice arrives at all, the claim
proceeds and the acquisition date stands.

If the counter-notice disputes the claim, it must say on what ground the company is not entitled
that the floor area exceeds the limit, that the membership was short, that a notice was
defective. At that point the claim stops unless the RTM company applies to the First-tier
Tribunal within two months of the counter-notice for a determination that it was entitled to
acquire the right. Miss those two months and the claim is treated as withdrawn.

## Costs

Until 3 March 2025 an RTM company was liable for the landlord's reasonable costs of dealing with
the claim, which made a failed claim expensive and was used to deter leaseholders from trying.
Section 50 of the Leasehold and Freehold Reform Act 2024 changed that, so each party now normally
bears its own costs, with limited exceptions where the tribunal decides otherwise.

If you are reading older guidance, articles or forum threads that warn about liability for the
landlord's legal bill, check the date on them.

## The acquisition date

Where the claim is not disputed, the acquisition date must be at least three months after the
deadline for the counter-notice. Where it is disputed and the tribunal decides in the company's
favour, the date runs from when that determination becomes final.

On that date the management functions under the leases transfer to the RTM company. The
freeholder stays the freeholder and keeps the ground rent, the lease terms do not change, and
forfeiture and consents to alterations remain with the landlord but repairs, maintenance,
insurance obligations and service charges become the company's job.

Three months feels generous and disappears quickly. Use it: appoint your agent or decide to
self-manage, open the accounts, get quotes for the building's insurance, and write to the
current managing agent about handover.
